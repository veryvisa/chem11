This is the brief on BC Chemistry 11. Extensive versus intensive properties. A gold ring and bar share the same density despite different masses. Which properties care about how much substance you have? First, extensive properties depend on amount.

Mass, volume, length, surface area, total mass. Intensive properties don't. Density, melting point, boiling point, color, temperature, viscosity. The half sample test. Cut your sample in half.

Value halves, extensive. Stays identical, intensive. Second, example one. Classify gold density, sheet surface area, water temperature, total student mass. Steps, half the gold has identical density, intensive.

Half the sheet has half the area, extensive. Half the water retains temperature, intensive. Fewer students lowers total mass, extensive. Answer, I-E-I-E. Example two.

Sample A is 38.6 grams, 2.0 milliliters. Sample B is 19.3 grams, 1.0 milliliters. Steps. A's density is 38.6 divided by 2.0, equaling 19.3 grams per milliliter. B's is 19.3 divided by 1.0, equaling 19.3 grams per milliliter.

Mass changed, so it's extensive. Density didn't, so it's intensive. Answer, D-A and D-B equal 19.3 grams per milliliter. Mass is extensive. Density is intensive.

Example three. Full kettles take twice as long to boil. Classify boiling point and total heat. Steps. Half the water boils at 100 degrees Celsius, intensive.

Half the water needs half the heat, extensive. Answer. Boiling point stays 100 degrees Celsius, intensive. Total heat needed is extensive. Finally, why is density intensive?

Bigger objects are heavier, so you might assume density grows too. The math. Double the sample. Mass goes to 2m. Volume to 2v.

Density is 2m divided by 2v. The 2s cancel, simplifying to m divided by v. Unchanged. Exam tip. On a list question, write E or I quickly using the half sample test.

Watch for total, always E, and for temperature, always I.
