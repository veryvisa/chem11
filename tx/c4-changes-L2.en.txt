This is the brief on physical versus chemical change. While melting chocolate and burning toast both happen in a kitchen with heat, only one creates something you can never turn back. First, chemical change means new substances form that you can't easily revert. Burning, rusting, cooking, tarnishing.

Physical change means no new substance, just altering form. Melting, freezing, condensing, evaporating, cutting, dissolving. The one real test. Is there a new substance afterwards? Clues like color, gas, precipitate, heat, or light need this test because boiling makes bubbles too.

Six phase changes, melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, sublimation, deposition, are physical. Solid H2O to liquid H2O is still water. Second, classify as physical or chemical. A. Steam condenses on a mirror. B. Paper burns to ash.

C. Chocolate melts. D. Silver spoon tarnishes. A. Still H2O, physical. B. Ash is new, chemical. C. Same stuff, physical.

D. Coating is new, chemical. Answer. A. Physical, B. Chemical. C. Physical, D. Chemical. Third, bubbles form in boiling water and when an antacid drops in cold water.

Classify each. Boiling bubbles are water vapor, physical. Tablet bubbles are new carbon dioxide gas, chemical. Answer. Boiling, physical.

Antacid tablet fizzing, chemical. Fourth, a lit candle. Wax turns liquid, travels up to wick and burns, giving heat, light, carbon dioxide, water vapor. Classify each. Melting is still wax, physical.

Moving up changes position, physical. Burning creates new substances, chemical. Answer. Melting and rising, physical. Burning, chemical.

Finally, ask. New substance or same substance in a new phase. New means chemical. Same means physical. Exam tip.

Say the new substance question in your head for every item. Any phase change word. Melts. Freezes. Condenses.

Evaporates. Boils. Means physical. Every time.
