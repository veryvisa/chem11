This is the brief on Chemistry 11, Qualitative-Quantitative Observation and Interpretation. First, let's lock in those key terms. Qualitative means no numbers. It relies purely on your senses. Quantitative means numbers from measuring or counting. So, according to the class definition, an observation is qualitative information gathered with your senses,

and it literally must be qualitative. Now, an interpretation is an explanation of what an observation means. It's based on evidence, not directly seen. A description is a statement that can be both qualitative and quantitative, because it lists properties using words and numbers.

Watch out for this test trap. In the statement, the reaction is exothermic because heat was released, you might lump it together, but look closer. Heat was released is the observation, whereas exothermic is your interpretation. Second, let's see how these definitions trick us.

A classic mistake is calling a count qualitative. If you say there are four beakers, well, that number actually makes it quantitative. Another slip-up, writing a conclusion under observations. Phrases like, a gas was produced or a chemical reaction occurred are actually interpretations. The correct raw observation is simply bubbles formed.

Also, avoid calling a measurement an observation. In this class, observations are strictly qualitative, so a reading like 46.5 milliliters is purely quantitative information. Finally, remember a specific difference regarding the handout. The class notes say an observation must be qualitative,

meaning measurements count as quantitative data, not observations. Many textbooks differ and call measurements quantitative observations. On this test, use the class version. Observation equals qualitative.
