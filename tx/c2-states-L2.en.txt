This is the brief on matter and its states. Push the plunger of a sealed syringe of air, it slides right in. Fill it with water, it won't budge. That one difference, how closely particles pack, explains everything about states of matter.

First, matter is anything with mass taking up space, or volume. Light, heat, and sound are energy. The four states are solid, liquid, gas, and plasma, a very hot, charged gas like lightning or the sun. Now, textbooks list three everyday states,

saying liquids are nearly incompressible. But your class handout lists four states, saying liquids are not compressible. For the test, use the handout. Four states and only gases are compressible.

Second, a worked example. Question, fill in shape and volume for a solid, liquid, and gas. An ice cube keeps its shape and size. Juice poured into a glass takes the container's shape, but stays exactly 250 milliliters.

Air spreads to fill the room. The answer, solid, definite shape, definite volume. Liquid, indefinite shape, definite volume. Gas, indefinite shape, indefinite volume. Finally, tight, fixed particles keep shape and volume.

That's a solid. Touching, sliding particles flow, changing shape while volume is fixed. A liquid. Far apart particles fill containers and compress. A gas.

Next question. Explain using particles why a sealed syringe of air can be pushed in, but water can't. Well, air particles are far apart with empty space. So, pushing the plunger moves them closer, compressing the gas.

Water particles already touch, with no room to squeeze. Answer. Gas particles are far apart with space to squeeze, while liquid particles already touch. Exam tip.

Memorize the 3x4 table of shape, volume, particle arrangement, and compressibility. It is often a fill in the blank. And use the words definite and indefinite exactly.
