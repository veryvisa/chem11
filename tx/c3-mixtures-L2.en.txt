This is the brief on pure substances, mixtures, and solutions. This material maps out the chemistry classification tree, showing you how to categorize any substance by asking two simple questions. First, let's hit the definitions. Homogeneous literally means one phase that looks the exact same everywhere.

Heterogeneous? Well, that means multiple visible phases. Pure substances have one fixed formula. Elements have one atom type, and compounds have more. Solutions contain a solute dissolved in a solvent, so their amounts can actually vary.

And, hey, every heterogeneous mixture is a mechanical mixture. Quick warning, textbooks call soil a mechanical mixture due to visible sand, but your class notes list it as a homogeneous mixture. On the test, definitely use the class answer. Second, let's test this.

Helium is one phase and one atom type, so an element. Sugar is one phase with a fixed formula, making it a compound. Vinegar is one phase with varying amounts, so it's a solution. Granola has multiple visible phases, right? That's a mechanical mixture.

Next up, pure versus salt water. Both look completely uniform, but pure water is a fixed compound. Salt water is a solution with variable amounts of a salt solute and a water solvent. Lastly, sand and iron. Visible particles mean it's absolutely a mechanical mixture.

A magnet easily separates the iron, proving they kept their own properties. Finally, distinguishing pure substances from solutions. Both look totally identical in one phase, but a pure substance has a fixed formula, while a solution's amounts change. The proof?

Evaporating a solution leaves the solute right behind. Exam tip. Expect to draw the classification tree from memory. Practice sketching all eight boxes in under a minute. For any substance, ask the questions in order.

Phases, fixed composition, types of atom.
