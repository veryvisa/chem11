This is the brief on Unit 4 Preview, Properties and Changes. This guide explains exactly what matter is, what it's like, and what it can turn into. First, physical versus chemical properties. Matter has mass and takes up space. Energy, like heat, isn't matter.

If a label says colorless liquid and highly flammable, what's the difference? One is what it's like, the other what it can become. Physical properties, like color, are observable without changing the substance. Chemical properties show how it reacts, forming new substances, like rusting. Try the quick test.

Can I check this by just looking or measuring and still have the same stuff? If yes, it's physical. Words like rusts mean chemical. Second, extensive and intensive are our two physical properties. A gold ring and a gold bar have very different masses, yet exactly the same density.

So which properties care about how much and which do not? Extensive depends on amount, like mass. Intensive does not, like density. The half sample test. Cut your sample in half.

Value halves, it's extensive. Stays the same, intensive. Finally, physical versus chemical change. Melting chocolate and burning toast both use heat, but only one makes something you can never turn back. Chemical changes form new substances, making them hard to reverse.

Physical changes form no new substance. The one real test. Is there a new substance afterwards? Color changes or heat are clues, but you need the real test, because boiling also makes bubbles. All six phase changes, melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, sublimation, deposition, are physical changes.

H2O solid going to H2O liquid is still water.
