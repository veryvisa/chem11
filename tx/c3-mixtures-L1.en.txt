This is the brief on pure substances, mixtures, and solutions. This review covers the essential chemistry definitions for classifying matter, followed by the common mistakes and specific traps you need to avoid on your test. First, key terms. Homogeneous means one phase and looks the same.

Heterogeneous means more than one phase. Pure means constant composition and one substance. Homogeneous mixture means variable composition and multiple substances. Another name for a homogeneous mixture is a solution. Another name for a heterogeneous mixture is a mechanical mixture.

In saltwater, the solute is salt getting dissolved. The solvent is water doing the dissolving. For soil, the class answer is homogeneous mixture, noting textbooks say heterogeneous. Elements and compounds are pure substances. An element has one type of atom.

A compound has more than one. Wait, if a compound has more than one type of atom, shouldn't it be a mixture? Second, common mistakes. First, calling every clear liquid pure. The fix?

Saltwater and soda pop look uniform, but are solutions, meaning mixtures. Next, putting compounds under mixtures because they contain multiple elements. The fix? A compound's fixed formula makes it pure. Last, mixing up solute and solvent.

The fix? The solvent is usually the larger amount doing the dissolving. Finally, test traps. One, seeing a clear liquid and assuming it equals pure. It could be a solution.

Two, seeing a compound's two elements and assuming it equals a mixture. A compound is pure due to its fixed formula. Three is the soil exception. Most textbooks call soil a heterogeneous mechanical mixture because you see bits of sand, clay, and plant matter.

On this test, if soil is asked, use the class answer. Homogeneous mixture.
