This is the brief on kinetic energy. Rotational, vibrational, and translational. Basically, kinetic energy is just the energy particles have because they're moving. So as temperature goes up, those particles move faster, meaning their kinetic energy goes up too. First, let's define the three types of motion from the class handout. Rotational is when the molecule just spins in place. Bond lengths and angles stay exactly the

same. Vibrational means the atoms actually shake, so bonds stretch and bend, changing those lengths and angles, but the molecule itself stays in the same place. Translational happens when the whole molecule moves from one place to another in a straight line until it hits something. Here, bond lengths and angles do not change. Now, translational energy matters most for phase changes, because particles need enough freedom to pull away from each other, meaning melting and

boiling happen when translational energy reaches a critical value. A quick note on this. Many textbooks say solid particles only vibrate, but the class handout and graph differ. For this test, keep the handout's version. A solid is mostly vibrating in place, and if asked what increases while a solid warms, give the class answer, which is vibrational plus some translational. Second, let's walk through the three worked examples. Example 1. The question. Name the type of motion for

A. A water molecule spinning in place, B. The bonds in a water molecule stretching and bending, and C. A gas molecule moving across a container. The steps. For A. Bonds are the same, position is the same, just spinning, means rotational. For B. Bonds change, position is the same, which means vibrational. For C. Bonds are the same, position changes, which means translational. The answer. A is rotational, B is vibrational, and C is translational. Example 2. The question. Liquid water is heated from 20

degrees Celsius to 80 degrees Celsius. What happens to the kinetic energy, and which type increases the most? The steps. Temperature rises, so molecules move faster, meaning kinetic energy increases. In a liquid, molecules already slide past each other, so the faster motion is mostly moving from place to place, meaning translational. The answer. Kinetic energy increases, translational increases the most. Example 3. The question. Walk through the water heating curve's five segments and explain

why translational energy decides melting and boiling. The steps. 1. Solid warming. They're packed close, mostly vibrating in place, and vibrating harder as energy is added. 2. Melting. They gain enough energy to break out of the solid arrangement and slide past each other. Temperature is constant. 3. Liquid warming. They move more freely and faster. Translational increases. 4. Boiling. There is enough energy to escape the liquid completely. Temperature is constant. 5. Gas warming. They're far

apart, racing around faster. The answer. Translational motion is the only one that moves molecules away from each other. Finally, let's address the hardest question. Rotational versus vibrational motion. You know, students mix them up all the time because the molecule stays in the same place for both. Just clarify it like this. If bonds are unchanged, it is rotational, meaning it's spinning. If bonds stretch or bend, it is vibrational. Exam tip. For any which motion question, ask two things. Do the bonds change?

Does the molecule move to a new place? For which matters most for phase change? The answer is always translational.
