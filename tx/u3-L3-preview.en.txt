This is the brief on BC Chemistry 11, Unit 3, Classifying Matter. First are five building blocks. An atom is the smallest particle of an element, keeping its properties, holding protons, neutrons, and electrons. Crazy enough, 50 million make one centimeter.

An element is a pure substance of only one type of atom. Remember, one type, not one atom, like O2, N2, H2, or gold. Molecules are two or more bonded atoms. Compounds are pure substances of different atom types, chemically combined in a fixed way, like H2O, NaCl, or NaHCO3.

Lastly, ions are charged particles with a plus or minus sign, NO3-, SO4-, 2-, or Na+. Second, we sort these using our first question, how many phases? Homogeneous means one phase, looking identical everywhere. Think air, water, salt water, iron.

Heterogeneous means multiple phases, where you see different parts, like gravel, a pencil, or a salad. Every heterogeneous mixture is literally a mechanical mixture, like sand with iron filings or trail mix. Finally, our second question only applies to homogeneous matter.

Fixed composition? Pure substances have fixed formulas, splitting into elements and compounds, like H2O, O2, Cu, and sugar. Homogeneous mixtures, or solutions, physically combine substances where amounts vary.

Salt water, soda pop. Basically, matter is categorized by identifying its atomic composition and walking down a two-question tree based on phases and fixed composition. In a solution, the solute is the substance that gets dissolved, salt, and the solvent does the dissolving, water.
