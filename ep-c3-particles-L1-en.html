Transcript讲稿文字

This is the brief on atom, element, molecule, compound, and ion key terms and common mistakes. First, the key terms. An element is a pure substance of only one type of atom, not one atom, like O2 or Cu. A molecule is two or more bonded atoms. A compound is two or more different types chemically combined. Spot an ion by a charge in the formula, like NO3- or SO42-. An atom is an element's smallest particle with its properties, made of protons, neutrons, and electrons. For class labels, O2 is M and E, CH4 is M and C, and NaCl is C. Second, let's fix common mistakes. Think an element must be a single atom? O2 and F2 have two atoms, but one type equals both element and molecule. Missing the charge? Always check for a plus or minus first. SO42- is an ion, not a compound. Write both labels when two apply, like H2O, CH4, and NH3 being M and C. Compounds need different types, so N2 isn't one. Finally, note textbook differences. The class key labels a lone symbol, like Fe or Cl, as an atom, O2 and F2 as both molecule and element, and NaCl as just a compound. Many textbooks would say Fe can also mean the element iron, and that NaCl is an ionic compound made of ions, not molecules, which is why NaCl gets only C, even though class notes say all compounds are molecules. On this test, use the class labels.
