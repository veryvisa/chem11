This is the brief on Unit 3 Review. Classifying Matter. First, element, pure substance of one type of atom, O2 Cu. Molecule versus compound. Molecule, 2 plus atoms bonded. Compound, 2 plus different types chemically combined. How do you spot an ion? Charge and formula, NO3 minus, SO42 minus. Atom, smallest particle of an element with its properties. Protons, neutrons, electrons. Class labels for O2CH4NACL. O2M slash E. CH4MC. NACLC. Mistakes. Don't assume element means single atom. O2F2 are both. Don't miss charges. Check for plus or minus first. Handout versus textbook. Class key labels FE or CL as atom. O2F2 as both. NACL as compound only. Textbooks say FE means element iron. NACL is made of ions. On this test, use the class labels. Second, homogeneous versus heterogeneous. Homogeneous, one phase. Heterogeneous, multiple phases. Pure substance versus homogeneous mixture. Pure, constant composition, one substance. Homogeneous mixture. Variable composition, two plus substances. Another name for a homogeneous mixture. For a heterogeneous mixture. Solution, mechanical mixture. Solute versus solvent in salt water. Solute, salt gets dissolved. Solvent, water does dissolving. Element versus compound on the tree. Both pure substances. Element, one atom type. Compound, multiple types. Mistakes. Don't call clear liquids pure. Salt water is a solution. Don't put compounds under mixtures just because they contain two plus elements. Fixed formulas make them pure. Finally, class answer, soil is a homogeneous mixture. Most textbooks call soil heterogeneous, mechanical mixture, because you see bits of sand, clay, plant matter, but your notes list soil as a homogeneous mixture. On this test, if soil is asked, use the class answer, homogeneous mixture.
