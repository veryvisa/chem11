This is the brief on physical versus chemical changes. A chemical change happens when a new substance forms, and it's really hard to get the original back. A physical change means no new substance forms. Only the shape, phase, or appearance changes. So all six phase changes, like melting or freezing, are strictly physical. Right, so things like a new color, bubbling gas, a precipitate, heat, or light are great clues of a chemical change, but you absolutely must confirm a new substance actually formed. Take a fried egg. That's chemical because you literally can't unfry it, meaning new substances definitively formed. Second, let's fix some common mistakes. Mistake one is calling boiling chemical because a gas is produced. The fix? Ask yourself what that gas is. It's just water vapor. The exact same substance, making it completely physical. Mistake two is assuming breaking glass or shredding paper is chemical because you can't undo it. Remember, being hard to reverse is only a clue. Since there's no new substance, it's purely physical. Mistake three is thinking dissolving sugar is chemical because it just disappears. It's still sugar. If you evaporate the water, the sugar comes right back. Reversibility and visual tricks are just clues, but the lack of a new substance is the absolute deciding factor. Finally, those test traps. Boiling makes gas, but calling it chemical is wrong. Water vapor is still water, so it's physical. Broken glass cannot be undone, meaning calling it chemical is incorrect. It is still glass, making it physical.
