This is the brief on the six phase changes, key terms and common mistakes. When a solid jumps straight to a gas, like dry ice, that's sublimation. From a gas directly to a solid, like frost, you've got deposition. Gas to liquid is condensation, like steam on a mirror, while liquid to gas is evaporation. Here's the golden rule. Any phase change is always physical. So which three actually absorb energy? The answer is melting, evaporation, and sublimation toward gas. Second, let's fix some common mistakes. It's easy to mix up sublimation and deposition, so just check the starting line. Sublimation starts as a solid, think dry ice, while deposition starts as a gas, like frost. Also, don't swap your liquid and gas terms. Condensation makes liquid drops, whereas evaporation makes gas. And please don't get caught calling frost freezing. Freezing starts from a liquid, but frost forms straight from water vapor, making it deposition. Finally, watch out for test traps. If they say frost is freezing, it's wrong. Frost is deposition, going from vapor to ice. Also, beware the handout versus textbook rule. Your class diagram names liquid to gas evaporation. Many textbooks call it vaporization, splitting it into evaporation of the surface below the boiling point, and boiling throughout the liquid at the boiling point. On this test, evaporation is the expected name. On the heating curve, the flat step at 100 degrees is called boiling.
