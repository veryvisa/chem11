This is the Brief on Chemistry 11, First Unit, in One Pass. This unit covers five key parts, from how scientists observe to the energy of moving particles, delivering the absolute essentials on matter, properties, and phase changes. First, numbers mean quantitative, ruling out observation. A theory says why. A law doesn't. Data supports never proves. Mass before a reaction, 45.6 grams. Mass after, 45.6 grams. Is that a hypothesis, data, a theory, or a law? Data. Packing explains states of matter. Push the plunger of a sealed syringe full of air and it slides in. Fill it with water and it won't budge. A glass of iced cola. How many phases? Steps. Count the visible parts. Ice, liquid, bubbles. Answer. Three. Second, check for a charge first. Read formulas. O2, CO2, NaCl, Fe, SO4, 2-. Steps. Write atom, element, molecule, compound, or ion beside each. Answer. O2 gets molecule, element. H2O gets molecule, compound. Four properties. Underline the verb. Extensive properties care about how much, like total mass. Intensive don't, like temperature. Melting chocolate and burning toast. Step. Ask new substance. Answer. Any phase change word is always physical. Finally, frost appears on a car window overnight without ever being liquid water. Steps. Draw the gas-solid-liquid triangle. Answer. Follow the arrow. The burner under a pot of ice water is on full, yet the thermometer is stuck at zero degrees Celsius for minutes. Steps. Ask where the heat is going. Answer. Energy breaks attractions and separates particles instead of increasing their speed. Motion is rotational, vibrational, translational, involving bond changes or moving to a new place. A water molecule can spin like a top, shake like a spring, or fly across the room. For which matters most for phase change, the answer is always translational.
